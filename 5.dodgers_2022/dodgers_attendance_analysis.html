# Run ANOVA for each categorical column

results = {}
for col in columns_to_check:
    # get unique non-null group values in a stable order
    groups_vals = df[col].dropna().unique()
    # build groups and drop groups with <=1 observation
    groups = [df.loc[df[col] == val, 'attend'].values for val in groups_vals]
    groups = [g for g in groups if g.size > 1]

    F, p = f_oneway(*groups)
    results[col] = {'F': F, 'p': p, 'n_groups': len(groups)}


# Nicely print results
for col, res in results.items():
    print(f"{col} → F = {res['F']:.5f}, p = {res['p']:.5f} (groups={res['n_groups']})")

Conclusion¶

Most factors show no significant difference in mean attendance (p > 0.05). Statistically significant effects are:

  • bobblehead — large positive effect on attendance (F = 40.444, p < 0.001).
  • day_of_week — attendance varies by weekday (F = 3.644, p = 0.003).
  • month — attendance varies by month (F = 2.567, p = 0.026).
  • temp_bin — attendance varies by temperature bin (F = 3.67202, p = 0.00868).

Binning temperature reduced p from 0.23 to ~0.0087, indicating a nonlinear effect: moderate temperatures increase attendance while extreme cold/hot reduce it.

Attendance for games is impacted by several factors. By gauging all possible factors, such as weather, promotional events and schedule, we can find variances to increase attendance.

Over the 2022 season, our attendance fluctuated between lows near 24,000 and highs of 56,000. These extremes are highly unlikely as they represent single data points. For example, opening day had high numbers, but we can't expect to repeat these data points each time.

We have little control over the weather or whether the game is played at night. Fortunately, these factors play a smaller role in attendance than other factors.

Promotional events such as giveaways and fireworks are within our control. Fireworks, shirts, and caps play little role in attendance. However, bobblehead giveaways have a significant impact on attendance.

For the schedule, Monday and Wednesday have lower attendance. Tuesday has the largest average attendance. Opponents also play a larger role in attendance, with the Angels, Mets, Nationals, and White Sox drawing the largest crowds. It is worth noting that we can't only play high attendance teams. Temperatures cannot be controlled but middle temperatures above 62 and below 86 draw higher attendance, as hot and cold temperatures deter attendance.

Overall guidance:

  • April, August, July, and June have the highest attendance. Scheduling more home games in these months could boost attendance.
  • May, October, and September have the lowest attendance. Either aim to improve these months with promotions or prepare for lower attendance.
  • Bobblehead giveaways significantly boost attendance. Consider scheduling more bobblehead giveaway games.
  • While they don't need to be completely cancelled, shirts, caps, and fireworks have little impact on attendance. Consider reducing these promotions to save costs.
  • Attendance is higher between 62 and 86 degrees. Temperatures outside this range deter attendance. While we can't control the weather, we can consider scheduling fewer home games during extreme temperatures.

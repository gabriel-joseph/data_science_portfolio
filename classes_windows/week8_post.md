In your own words, describe what t-testing is.

T-Testing is the comparison between the means of two groups after hypothesis testing. The testing is broken into 3 categories. One-sample t-Test, Two-sample t-Test, and Paired t-Test.

One-sample t-Test is a hypothesis used to determine whether a mean different from a value. Suppose we knew a scoop of protein powder contained 10 grams of protein. If we opened a random case of protein powder and measured out 1 scoop, we would expect the scoop contains about 10g of protein. We could repeat this test with 10 other cases of protein and verify that they always contain 10g of protein.

Two-sample t-Test compares two separate groups to see their comparisons. Imagine we have group A with 50 people and group B with 50 people. Both groups are given the same meals and do the same exercise regimen, but group B is given 3 servings of protein powder every day. After 1 month, we would measure the mean muslce mass of group A and group B. If the protein powder is effective, we would expect group B to have more more muscle mass than group A. This type of experimenation is commonly known as the placebo group. 

Paired tests are when we evaluate a group means before and after a hypothesis. Company "Z" asserts that muscle mass increases 5% in 1 month if customers consume at least 3 scoops of protein powder per day. To test the hypothesis, we gather 100 adults, measure their initial muscle mass, allow them to consume 3 scoops of protein powder every day for a month, and measure their final muscle mass. The difference between the mean of the group after 1-month vs 1-month before will confirm whether or not the hypothesis was true.
